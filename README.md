# ZK-OPA: Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Authorization for Microservices

This research proposes **ZK-OPA**, a decentralized authorization system that combines Open Policy Agent (OPA) with Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs) to enable fine-grained, privacy-preserving access control across microservices, particularly in banking environments. A trusted issuer (the bank's identity/KYC system) signs a credential over the customer's attributes. At login, the client proves once, in zero knowledge, that its hidden attributes satisfy the subject part of an OPA policy, and binds that proof to a session key. Each microservice verifies the proof with public keys and evaluates the remaining request- and resource-dependent part of the policy in a local OPA. Shared infrastructure therefore holds only public commitments (policy hashes, revocation roots, verification keys): no central decision service sits in the request path, and no service on that path ever sees sensitive attributes such as income, risk score, or KYC level. Signed tokens with locally evaluated OPA already avoid the central call, so the contribution is the second property. The research will investigate how to translate a provable subset of Rego, OPA's policy language, into ZK circuits, along with revocation, replay protection, and dynamic policies. **The primary objective is to evaluate what this privacy costs in proving time, request latency, and policy expressiveness compared to centralized authorization, locally evaluated OPA, and asymmetric signed tokens.**
