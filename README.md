# ZK-OPA: Privacy-Preserving Decentralized Authorization for Microservices

This research proposes **ZK-OPA**, a decentralized authorization system in which a Zero-Knowledge Proof (ZKP) is the access decision itself. It targets fine-grained, privacy-preserving access control across microservices, particularly in banking environments. A trusted issuer (the bank's identity/KYC system) signs a credential over the customer's attributes. Policies are written in Rego, the language of Open Policy Agent (OPA), and compiled ahead of time into ZK circuits. OPA runs only at compile time and is not present in the services.

At login, the client proves once that it holds a valid, unrevoked credential and commits to its hidden attributes. On each request, the client proves that the committed attributes, together with that specific request, satisfy the whole policy. The service builds the public inputs itself (method, path, amount, resource, time, policy hash, revocation root), so a proof made for one request fails for any other. The service then verifies the proof in-process with a public verifying key. There is no policy engine at runtime, no central decision service, and no third-party call in the request path. Verification takes the same small, fixed time however complex the policy is. Shared infrastructure holds only public commitments (verifying keys, policy hashes, revocation roots), and services pull them in the background without blocking requests. No service ever sees sensitive attributes such as income, risk score, or KYC level.

The research will investigate how to translate a provable subset of Rego into ZK circuits, how to split proving into a heavy login proof and a small per-request proof, and how to bring server-held data (such as balances or account ownership) into the proof through signed attestations. It also covers revocation, replay protection, and dynamic policies. Moving the decision into the proof shifts the cost from the service to the client, which must generate a proof for every request. **The primary objective is to evaluate what this costs in per-request proving time, end-to-end latency, and policy expressiveness compared to a hybrid design (proof at login plus local OPA), locally evaluated OPA with signed tokens, and centralized OPA.**
